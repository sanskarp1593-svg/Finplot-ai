# Record-Keeping Requirements for Small Businesses

## Why Keep Records?
Proper record-keeping is legally required and practically essential:
- Income Tax Act requires maintenance of books of accounts
- GST Act requires records of all supplies, purchases, and tax collected/paid
- Records serve as evidence during audits and assessments

## Who Must Keep Books?
Under Section 44AA of the Income Tax Act:
- **Business**: If income exceeds ₹2,50,000 or turnover exceeds ₹25,00,000 in any of the three preceding years
- **Profession**: Always mandatory for specified professions (doctors, lawyers, architects, engineers, accountants, etc.)
- In practice, every business should maintain books regardless of threshold

## Minimum Records to Maintain
1. **Cash book**: All cash receipts and payments, daily
2. **Journal/ledger**: Double-entry bookkeeping of all transactions
3. **Purchase register**: All purchases with invoices
4. **Sales register**: All sales with invoices
5. **Stock register**: Opening stock, purchases, sales, closing stock (for goods businesses)
6. **Bank statements**: All bank transactions, reconciled monthly
7. **GST records**: Outward and inward supply details, input tax credit register
8. **Salary register**: Salary, TDS, PF/ESI details for employees

## Retention Period
- **Income Tax**: Books must be preserved for **6 years** from the end of the relevant assessment year (8 years if a case is pending)
- **GST**: Records must be retained for **72 months** (6 years) from the due date of filing the annual return for that year
- **Practical recommendation**: Keep records for at least 8 years to cover all eventualities

## Digital Record-Keeping
- Electronic records are valid and admissible if they can be produced in printed form
- Use accounting software to maintain records digitally
- Regular backups are essential – the law doesn't excuse data loss
- Cloud storage is acceptable but ensure data residency compliance

## Tax Audit
- Businesses with turnover > ₹1 crore (₹10 crore if cash transactions are ≤ 5% of turnover) must get accounts audited under Section 44AB
- Audit report must be filed by 30 September of the assessment year
