# Digital Payment Compliance for Small Businesses

## Mandatory Digital Payment Acceptance
As per Section 269SU of the Income Tax Act (effective 1 Feb 2020), businesses with annual turnover exceeding ₹50 crore must provide digital payment facilities (UPI, debit card, BHIM-UPI, etc.) with no surcharge or MDR.

## Relevance to Small Businesses
While the ₹50 crore threshold exempts most small businesses, accepting digital payments is highly recommended:
- No MDR (Merchant Discount Rate) on UPI payments for small merchants
- Government incentive schemes for digital adoption
- Builds transparent transaction records for GST and income-tax compliance

## Cash Transaction Limits
- **Section 269ST**: No person can receive ₹2,00,000 or more in cash in a single transaction, or from a single person in a day, or in respect of a single event/occasion. Penalty = 100% of the amount received.
- **Section 40A(3)**: Business expenses paid in cash exceeding ₹10,000 in a single day to a single person are disallowed as a deduction. (Limit is ₹35,000 for transporters.)
- **Section 269SS/269T**: Loans and deposits of ₹20,000 or more must be by cheque/bank transfer, not cash.

## Record-Keeping for Digital Payments
- Maintain a separate record of UPI/card/wallet payments received
- Keep settlement statements from payment gateways
- Reconcile digital payment receipts with bank statements monthly
- Retain records for at least 8 years from the end of the relevant assessment year

## GST and Digital Payments
- Digital payments must be accounted for in GST returns the same as cash payments
- E-invoicing is mandatory for businesses above ₹5 crore turnover (threshold may change)
