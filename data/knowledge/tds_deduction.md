# TDS Deduction Rules for Small Businesses

## What is TDS?
Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) is an advance tax collected by the payer at the time of making certain payments. The deducted amount is deposited with the government on behalf of the payee.

## When Must a Small Business Deduct TDS?
A business (not an individual/HUF without audit obligation) must deduct TDS on:
- **Salary payments** (Section 192): As per income-tax slab of the employee
- **Contractor/sub-contractor payments** (Section 194C): 1% (individual/HUF) or 2% (others) if single payment > ₹30,000 or total in a year > ₹1,00,000
- **Professional/technical fees** (Section 194J): 2% for technical services, 10% for professional services, if payment > ₹30,000/year
- **Rent** (Section 194I): 2% for plant/machinery, 10% for land/building, if rent > ₹2,40,000/year
- **Commission/brokerage** (Section 194H): 5% if payment > ₹15,000/year

## TDS on Payments to Residents (Section 194Q)
Businesses with turnover > ₹10 crore must deduct 0.1% TDS on purchase of goods exceeding ₹50 lakh from a single seller in a year.

## TDS Return Filing
- Quarterly returns in Form 26Q (non-salary) and 24Q (salary)
- Due dates: 31 Jul, 31 Oct, 31 Jan, 31 May for Q1–Q4

## Penalties
- Late deduction: interest at 1% per month
- Late deposit: interest at 1.5% per month
- Late filing of return: ₹200 per day (max = TDS amount)

## TAN Requirement
Every business deducting TDS must obtain a Tax Deduction Account Number (TAN) and quote it in all TDS-related documents.
